# Appendix A: The Trust Debugger

Use the full Debugger on significant, stalled or confusing threads. For routine exchanges, the short version is enough: **evidence → open question → move → stop condition.** Never delay a straightforward answer to fill in a worksheet.

**Thread / date:** ______________________ **Exact last buyer signal:** ______________________________

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What does the buyer believe right now? (evidence, not mind-reading) | |
| 2 | What don't they believe or understand yet? | |
| 3 | What are they protecting? (time, money, reputation, capacity…) | |
| 4 | Which gate is open? (write the question, not just the number) | |
| 5 | What's their likely posture, and one alternative reading? | |
| 6 | P(outcome): strong / weak / unknown, and why | |
| 7 | What value is relevant, in their words? | |
| 8 | What perceived risk or hard constraint is present? | |
| 9 | Last trust deposit? | |
| 10 | Last trust withdrawal? | |
| 11 | What signal is missing? | |
| 12 | Smallest appropriate move (including pause, decline, stop)? | |
| 13 | **What should I NOT do?** | |

**The Pause Test:** Am I sending this because it helps the buyer, or because I'm uncomfortable with the silence?

**What response would change my diagnosis?** ______________________________________________

<div class="example" markdown="1">
**Completed example.** A buyer says the service looks useful but asks whether their team must attend weekly sessions. Earlier, they mentioned a major launch. *Evidence supports:* interest plus a concern about participation. *Does not establish:* fear of investing, or a price objection. *Gate:* G4 (what does this require of us?). *TU:* Value strong; P(outcome) unknown; Risk weak (capacity). *Missing signal:* an honest participation schedule. *Move:* Clarifier, then a timing decision if needed. *Do NOT:* send another testimonial or promise it'll be effortless.

# Appendix B: Six Gate Reference Sheet

| Gate | Buyer's question | Signals | Increases trust | Decreases trust | First moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1 Reality** | Is this person real and coherent? | "Do we know each other?" Profile check | Accurate identity, truthful context, coherent profile | Fake familiarity, vague affiliation, profile contradiction | Clarifier, Self-Disclosure |
| **G2 Relevance** | Does this matter to me? | "Why are you asking?" "Not my area" | Specific observation; a question that can be answered "no" | Diagnosing from a job title | Clarifier, Insight Gift |
| **G3 Fit** | Do they understand my situation? | Corrections, "we're different" | Accurate recap, meaningful distinction | Forcing a standard diagnosis | Mirror, Clarifier |
| **G4 Safety** | Is it safe to explore? | "What would you need from us?" | Clear time, inputs, boundaries, exit | Surprise commitments, reassurance adjectives | Clarifier |
| **G5 Value** | Is it worth the total investment? | Price, alternatives, "why not DIY?" | Real proof, clear scope, honest alternatives | Inflated ROI, hidden price | Story, Clarifier |
| **G6 Identity** | Is this the right decision for us? | Approvals, ownership, final reservations | Precise recap, explicit responsibilities, space | Identity pressure, bypassing approvers | Mirror, Clarifier |

**Remember:** gates can be passed before the DM (via content and profile) and can reopen (gate regression). Use the earliest open gate that blocks the next step. One primary trust job per touch, while answering every material question honestly.

# Appendix C: Trust Utility Worksheet

**Trust Utility = P(outcome) × Value − Perceived Risk.** A qualitative diagnostic. Never compute a number.

| Term | Rating (strong / weak / unknown) | Evidence from this conversation | What's still unknown? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P(outcome):** can it work *for them*? | | | Capability, mechanism, fit or implementation? |
| **Value:** does it matter to them, now? | | | Compared with what? |
| **Perceived risk:** what might they lose or have to put in? | | | Information gap, or a real constraint? |

- **Likely bottleneck:** ____________________________
- **A question that would test it:** ____________________________
- **A proportionate signal or scope change:** ____________________________
- **An alternative that may serve them better:** ____________________________
- **What would justify pausing or declining?** ____________________________

**Calculation discipline:** if the buyer gives you numbers, record the source and assumptions and calculate only what the inputs support. Five unsuitable 30-minute calls = 2.5 hours in calls, not 2.5 hours of recoverable profit. Keep capacity freed, potential revenue, signed value and cash collected separate.

# Appendix D: Five Trust Moves Matrix

| Move | Purpose | Use when | Best gates | Misuse to catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mirror** | Make understanding visible and correctable | Hesitation, feeling misunderstood, resistance | G3, G6 | Invented emotion; mechanical repetition; "…which is why you need us" |
| **Clarifier** | Reduce a specific ambiguity | Process, terms, scope or next step unclear | G1, G2, G4, G5 | Lectures; jargon; limits buried at the end |
| **Insight Gift** | A useful, bounded way to see the issue | Curiosity; control resistance; they need evidence of thinking | G2, G3 | Baited gifts; implied debt; speculative full audits |
| **Story** | Ground a mechanism or trade-off | They can't picture how it works | G5 | Fabricated proof; irrelevant success stories |
| **Self-Disclosure** | State a relevant truth or limit | Skepticism from polished language | G1, G3 | Manufactured vulnerability; asking for sympathy |

# Appendix E: Signal Recognition Cheat Sheet

**Three different classifications. Don't mix them up.**
**State** = how they're engaging (Ch 4). **Gate** = what they're deciding (Ch 5). **Rung** = what they're doing (Ch 10).

| Signal | Plausible state | Likely gate | Ladder rung | Check before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Like / view | Unknown | — | Attention | Nothing is established |
| "Thanks!" | Courtesy | G1–G2 | Attention | Was any business issue raised? |
| "Interesting." | Curious / polite | G2 | Curiosity | Two-Hypothesis Reply |
| Questions about the method | Curious / exploring | G2–G3 | Interest | General, or about *their* situation? |
| Detailed context, unprompted | Exploring | G3 | Exploration | Does it show a problem your work fits? |
| Questions about proof | Skeptical / evaluating | G3–G5 | Exploration | Which claim or similarity is in doubt? |
| Questions about workload or process | Hesitant / planning | G4 | Intent | Is capacity available, or just unclear? |
| Questions about price, terms, start | Evaluating / ready | G5–G6 | Intent → Readiness | Who decides? What's still open? |
| "I need to think about it." | Hesitant | Any | Varies | Which TU term? One gentle clarification |
| Silence | Several possible | — | — | What was agreed? Is another contact justified? |
| Clear refusal | A boundary | — | — | **Stop.** It's not a hidden buying signal |

**Confidence labels:** directly stated · strongly suggested · plausible · unknown.

# Appendix G: Conversation Rehydration Map

| Check | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Did they ask not to be contacted? | Stop. Keep the boundary. | Keep checking. No refusal isn't permission. |
| Did they invite a revisit (date or milestone)? | Return when it's met. | Look for material new context; elapsed time is weak. |
| Is the new context genuinely connected? | Name it briefly. | Don't invent a reason. |
| Can the message be low-effort and easy to decline? | Small status question or relevant update. | Shrink the ask, or don't send. |
| Do they decline or leave no next step? | Close the record. | Record the new agreement. |

**Draft from memory, not pretense:**
What we actually discussed: ______________________ What was agreed about future contact: ______________________
Why a return is relevant now: ______________________ Smallest useful question: ______________________ Stopping point after this: ______________________

**Five qualities of a good rehydration:** contextual · low-pressure · relevant · easy to ignore · consistent in tone.

# Appendix H: Modality Readiness Checklist

- ☐ I can say which question this format resolves better than text.
- ☐ The buyer asked for or agreed to the format.
- ☐ Length and preparation are proportionate.
- ☐ A written alternative exists where needed.
- ☐ Nothing sensitive is on screen or in the recording.
- ☐ Any recording is agreed before it starts.
- ☐ For calls: purpose, participants, time zone and output are clear.
- ☐ Material promises will be confirmed in writing.
- ☐ Declining the format won't become a reason to pressure them.

**Coherence Check before any escalation:** Profile ✓ · Content ✓ · Conversation tone ✓ · Offer follows the problem ✓ · Format fits trust level ✓ · Promise believable ✓

*Text → voice → video → call is not a mandatory ladder. Use the lowest-friction format that supports an accurate decision.*

# Appendix J: Daily LinkedIn DM Operating System

**Scan → Sort → Diagnose → Choose → Move → Record → Rehydrate**

**Priority:** my promises → meaningful replies → justified follow-ups → rehydration → new outreach.
**Time box:** 30–45 minutes. Never a sending quota.

| Step | Question |
|---|---|
| Scan | What did buyers actually say or do? What did I promise? |
| Sort | Which state is each thread in? (evaluation · exploring · promised · invited revisit · peer · dormant · inactive · do-not-contact) |
| Diagnose | Which state, gate and weakest TU term? |
| Choose | Which signal is missing, and which move supplies it (or pause)? |
| Move | One primary job, the right format |
| Record | What changed? Next action or stop condition? |
| Rehydrate | Is there an invited trigger or genuine new context? |

**Trust Graph Audit row:** Person · Relationship type · Last meaningful exchange + date · What's established · What's open · What I promised · Permitted next action or stop.

**Weekly review:** unique contacts · unique replies (refusals separate) · substantive conversations · qualified · evaluations · proposals · outcomes · time spent. Counts next to percentages. One change per week.

# Appendix K: Common Trust-Leaking Behaviors

| Trust leak | Corrective action |
|---|---|
| Fabricated familiarity | State the real relationship and why you're contacting them |
| Generic praise before a pitch | Use a relevant observation, or drop the praise |
| Diagnosing from a public clue | Separate the fact, the hypothesis and the question |
| Overloaded first message | One reason to talk, one manageable response |
| "What's your biggest challenge?" | Ask about one observable situation |
| Treating "thanks" as intent | Distinguish courtesy, interest, problem and evaluation |
| Interrogation (budget, authority, revenue at once) | Explain why a detail matters; ask in proportion |
| Free full engagement before fit | Bounded contribution; paid diagnosis for real investigation |
| Hiding price to protect the meeting | State price, range or pricing drivers |
| Manufactured urgency or scarcity | Use real timing and real constraints only |
| Following the sequence, not the conversation | Let the live thread override the plan |
| Unrequested audit or long video | Ask first; keep it bounded |
| Surprise meeting, participants or recording | Agree format, purpose and conditions first |
| Identity pressure ("serious founders invest") | Let the buyer's own criteria decide |
| **Hiding**: no clear offer for a ready buyer | Recap → reason → request, with price |
| Irrelevant or exaggerated proof | Explain the mechanism and the limits of comparability |
| Sounding unlike yourself ("crushing it," "quick brain-pick") | Read it aloud; say it like a respected professional would |
| Guilt-based close-outs | Close respectfully, once |
| Contacting after a no | Stop. Never switch channel or person to get around it |
| Promises lost in delivery | Transfer scope, dependencies and commitments explicitly |

**The ethical floor:** no faked compliments, manufactured urgency, exploited vulnerability, false scarcity, fabricated proof, implied endorsements or fake personalization. Trust is not a technique for bypassing consent.
