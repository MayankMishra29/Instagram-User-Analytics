## Post 1: The origin story (launch)

We had a 46% reply rate on LinkedIn outbound.

11,466 cold DMs. A five-person team. 30–35% of connection requests accepted.

By most outbound advice, that's the job done.

It wasn't.

The replies weren't turning into bookings. Not even close to what the reply rate promised.

So I stopped reading the dashboard and started reading the threads.

Here's what I kept seeing (including in my own messages):

Prospect: "Interesting, what do you do exactly?"
Us: [offer] + [proof] + [link] + "Open to a quick call this week?"

They asked ONE question.
We answered five they hadn't asked, then asked for 30 minutes of their week.

Then they went quiet. And the follow-up sequence fired on a timer, as if nothing had happened.

The problem wasn't volume. It wasn't even copy.

It was that we kept answering trust questions the buyer hadn't reached yet.

## Post 2: The Six Trust Gates

Every buyer in your DMs is silently answering one of six questions:

1. Reality: "Is this person real?"
2. Relevance: "Is this relevant to me?"
3. Fit: "Do they actually understand my situation?"
4. Safety: "Is it safe to explore this?"
5. Value: "Is it worth the investment?"
6. Identity: "Is this the right decision for us?"

Most "salesy" DMs aren't salesy because of the words.

They're salesy because they answer question 5 to someone still on question 2.

"Hey, we help founders get 10–20 qualified leads a month, open to a quick call?"

That's a Value pitch plus a Commitment ask, sent to someone who hasn't decided you're relevant.

Before your next message, ask: which question is this person actually on?

Then answer that one. Only that one.

## Post 3: Reply rates lie (B2B authority)

If your outbound agency reports reply rate, ask them what happens next.

Reply rate measures attention. Not readiness.

We ran a 46% reply rate across 11,466 DMs. The bottleneck wasn't getting replies, it was turning them into real bookings.

Here's what I'd ask anyone running outbound for you:

→ What do you report besides reply rate?
→ How do you choose who to contact, and why NOW?
→ What happens when a prospect replies "interesting"?
→ How many threads go quiet right after you ask for a call?
→ Show me three real threads, including one that didn't convert.

The dashboard tells you where it's leaking.
Only the conversations tell you why.

## Post 4: The Pause Test

The most useful sales question I know isn't about what to say.

It's this:

"Am I sending this because it helps the buyer, or because I'm uncomfortable with the silence?"

Most follow-ups fail it.

The "just bumping this up."
The unrequested case study.
The call request because you don't know what else to say.
The fake deadline.

Silence feels like rejection, so we add pressure. Pressure gives the buyer more reason to withdraw. We read that as needing MORE pressure.

That loop starts inside the seller, not the buyer.

Silence is data. Ambiguous data. It can mean busy, evaluating, uncertain or not interested.

The skill isn't guessing which.
It's choosing a move that's respectful under every reading, including sending nothing at all.

## Post 5: Interest isn't readiness

"Interesting."
"How would that work?"
"What would you recommend for us?"
"Can you send scope and pricing?"
"We have approval, what do you need to start?"

Five replies. Five completely different buyers.

The first one is curious. The last one is ready.

Most reps treat them all the same, usually by asking the curious one for a call and sending the ready one another "helpful resource."

Both lose the deal:
→ Push the curious buyer and they retreat.
→ Keep educating the ready buyer and they get confused, then go elsewhere.

Match the rung.
Curious gets a short, useful answer.
Ready gets a clear, priced offer.
