# Is Your LinkedIn Producing Pipeline, or Just Posts?

Most B2B founders and revenue leaders can tell you their follower count, their best-performing post and their average impressions.

Very few can tell you how many **qualified opportunities** LinkedIn produced last quarter, or why the ones it didn't produce stalled.

I've spent this year building demand the unglamorous way. I ran growth for an expert marketplace that reached **12.5k users in three months**, with a **46% reply rate across 11,466 cold LinkedIn DMs**. The biggest lesson: **attention was never the constraint. Relevance and the path from attention to a conversation were.** Reach, likes and impressions are leaky buckets. They measure the crowd, not who walked in the door.

This audit is the diagnostic I use to find where a LinkedIn presence is leaking. It takes about 20 minutes. Be honest. The value is in the low scores.

## How to score

For each check, score **0** (no / don't know), **1** (partly / inconsistently) or **2** (yes, and you could show someone the evidence). Add up each section (max 10) and the total (max 60).

# Section 1: Audience and Offer

*Does your content know who it's for, and connect to something you sell?*

| # | Check | Score (0–2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | You can state your buyer in one sentence: "We help [role + business context] dealing with [problem] when [trigger]." | |
| 1.2 | Most of your last ten posts are about problems that sit next to a service you actually sell. | |
| 1.3 | You've written down who is **out** of scope, and your content doesn't attract them by default. | |
| 1.4 | You collect your buyers' exact words (from calls, emails, comments) and use them in posts. | |
| 1.5 | A stranger could explain your offer back to you after reading three of your posts. | |
| | **Section total (max 10)** | |

**If you scored ≤ 5:** stop increasing your posting frequency. More posts will amplify the confusion. Write the buyer situation statement first.

# Section 2: Profile and Conversion Path

*When a relevant reader clicks your name, does the profile confirm what the post implied?*

| # | Check | Score (0–2) |

| 2.1 | Your headline names the audience, the problem or outcome, and your method or role. | |
| 2.2 | Your About section opens with the buyer's situation, not your biography. | |

# Section 4: CTAs and Lead Magnets

*Does each post ask for the right next step?*

| # | Check | Score (0–2) |
|---|---|---|

| 4.3 | Your lead magnet solves a small, real problem right next to your paid service. | |

# Section 5: Conversations

*When the right person engages, what happens next?*

| # | Check | Score (0–2) |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | You reply to the **substance** of relevant comments, not with "Thanks! DM me." | |
| 5.2 | You move to DM only when invited or when it's genuinely easier, and you carry the context over. | |
| 5.3 | Your DMs respond to what the person actually said, rather than following a pre-written sequence. | |
| 5.4 | You answer price questions directly (or give honest pricing logic) instead of forcing a call. | |
| 5.5 | You stop after a clear no, and your records keep that boundary. | |
| | **Section total (max 10)** | |

**If you scored ≤ 5:** your content may be working and your conversations leaking. That was exactly my lesson at a 46% reply rate: the replies came, and the bottleneck was what happened after them.

# Section 6: Measurement

*Can you tell what LinkedIn actually produces?*

| # | Check | Score (0–2) |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | You track substantive conversations and fit conversations, separately from reactions and comments. | |
| 6.2 | You define a qualified opportunity (problem, fit, decision path, agreed next step) and apply it consistently. | |
| 6.3 | You ask every new prospect how they found you, and record content as **sourced** or **influenced**. | |
| 6.4 | Your rates use explicit denominators, and you show counts next to percentages. | |
| 6.5 | You change one variable per cycle, so you can tell what worked. | |

## What to fix first

**Fix the earliest low-scoring section, not the most exciting one.** A great hook can't rescue a vague offer. A great lead magnet can't rescue a confusing profile. Great content can't rescue conversations that push, stall or hide the price.

| If your lowest section is… | Your first move |
|---|---|
| **1. Audience and offer** | Write the buyer situation statement and an out-of-scope list. Pause new content formats until it's done. |
| **2. Profile** | Rewrite the headline and About around the buyer's situation; test every Featured link on your phone. |
| **3. Content** | Plan next month with all four post jobs; add one evidence-based authority post a week. |
| **4. CTAs** | Swap engagement bait for application or experience CTAs; rebuild the lead magnet backward from your paid service. |
| **5. Conversations** | Stop templated follow-ups; reply to substance; state your price. |
| **6. Measurement** | Start a simple opportunity log with sourced vs. influenced, and ask every prospect how they found you. |
