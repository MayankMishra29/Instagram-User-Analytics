## Post 2: Viral ≠ valuable (Job: authority · Engine: contrarian · CTA: experience)

A post with 200,000 impressions can generate zero clients.

A post with 18,000 can generate three.

The second one is more valuable. Obviously.

So why do we keep optimizing for the first?

Because the dashboard counts the crowd, not who walked in the door.

Here's the shift that changed how I write:

Stop asking "How do I get more views?"
Start asking "How do I get attention from people who have a reason to buy?"

In practice, that means:

→ Write for a smaller, better audience ("SaaS founders with founder-led sales," not "founders")
→ Prove your thinking instead of claiming expertise
→ End with the smallest next step the post has earned

## Post 3: The rehook (Job: authority · Engine: how-to · CTA: application)

Most people obsess over line one.

The best LinkedIn writers engineer lines one to three as a unit.

Line 1 (hook) earns a glance.
Lines 2–3 (rehook) earn the read.

The rehook's job is to ADD information:

Narrow → "This matters most when the founder still approves every proposal."
Complicate → "The team is following the process exactly. The process is missing the decision."
Preview → "Here are three places to check before you change your price."

What doesn't work: "You won't believe what happens next." It adds nothing.

Try it: take your next post's hook and write three rehooks, one of each type. Keep the one that adds the most meaning.

## Post 4: The audit (Job: conversion, resource · Engine: list · CTA: resource)

Is your LinkedIn producing pipeline, or just posts?

Quick check. Score yourself honestly:

1. Can a stranger explain your offer after reading three of your posts?
2. Does your profile headline name your buyer and their problem?
3. Did last month include attraction, authority, relationship AND conversion posts?
4. Do your CTAs match where the reader is, or does everything end with "DM me"?
5. Do you reply to the substance of comments, or with "Thanks! DM me"?
6. Can you say how many qualified opportunities LinkedIn produced last quarter?

If you said no to three or more, posting more won't fix it.

## Post 5: CTAs (Job: authority → conversion · Engine: mistake + repair · CTA: commercial)

"Agree?"
"Thoughts?"
"Comment YES."

These CTAs ask for effort without giving a reason.

And they tell you nothing about who actually wants to buy.

Try a CTA ladder instead. Climb only as far as the post has earned:

1. Application: "Check one stalled proposal for an owner, a decision and a date."
2. Experience: "What's the biggest bottleneck you're seeing?"
3. Resource: "The review sheet is linked below. No email needed."
4. Conversation: "If you're unsure which it is, message me the stage where it stalls."
5. Commercial: "If you're solving this this quarter, message me."
