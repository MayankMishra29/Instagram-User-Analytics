# Why I Wrote This: Reach Is the Wrong Finish Line

I'm not a LinkedIn influencer. Most of my posts reach a few hundred people.

What I've done is build demand. I scaled two EdTech platforms on organic growth. This year I ran growth for an expert marketplace that reached **12.5k users in three months**. The first 1,000 sign-ups took months. **The next 1,000 came in seven days.**

Working with more than a thousand consultants and coaches trying to turn their expertise into clients, I noticed something that changed how I think about LinkedIn:

**Reach didn't predict results.**

The accounts with the biggest followings often converted *worse* than people with a few thousand engaged followers. And one of the cleanest wins I saw had nothing to do with virality. An expert added a booking link to a single, specific post that explained one problem for one kind of buyer, and got a booking from it. No funnel. No sequence.

That's the idea behind this guide:

> **Virality is the distribution engine. Relevance, authority and conversion are the lead engine.**

The goal isn't to get 100,000 people to like your post. It's to get the right 100 to 1,000 people to think: *"This person understands my problem. I should talk to them."*

This Field Guide gives you the core of the method, and you can use it on your next post today.

*Mayank Mishra, Founder, ThriveXLabs*

# 1. Viral ≠ Valuable

A post can get **200,000 impressions, 5,000 likes and 700 comments**, and generate zero clients.

Another can get **18,000 impressions, 300 likes and 40 comments**, and generate three ideal clients.

*(Illustrative numbers, but every B2B operator has seen this pattern.)*

The second post is more valuable. So the question isn't *"How do I get more views?"* It's:

> **How do I engineer attention from people who have a reason to buy?**

| Vanity LinkedIn | Business LinkedIn |
|---|---|
| More impressions | More *relevant* impressions |
| More likes | More authority with the right people |
| More followers | More profile visits from potential buyers |
| More comments | More qualified conversations |

**Optimize for qualified attention**: attention from people whose situation makes your expertise relevant.

</section>

# 2. The Anatomy of a Post That Converts

<div class="flow">
<span><b>Hook</b><br>Stop the right reader</span><span><b>Rehook</b><br>Make the next line necessary</span><span><b>Body</b><br>Deliver the value</span><span><b>Payoff</b><br>Keep the promise</span><span><b>CTA</b><br>A proportionate next step</span>
</div>

And the five things every post needs, **V.I.R.A.L.**:

| | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Voice** | Does this sound like a human who understands the work? |
| **Intrigue** | Would the right reader need the next line? |
| **Relevance** | Would my ideal buyer recognize themselves? |
| **Authority** | What gives them grounds to believe me? |
| **Lesson** | What can they now do or decide? |

**The promise–payoff contract:** the hook makes a promise, and the body keeps it. Never withhold the promised insight to force a DM. Give the insight away, and offer help with *implementation*.

</section>

<section class="chapter">

# 3. The Hook and the Rehook

Most people obsess over line one. Experts write lines one to three as a unit.

<div class="post" markdown="1">
I almost quit my business.

Not because it was failing.

Because it was succeeding in the wrong way.
</div>

The **hook** earns a glance. The **rehook** earns the read, because it adds information: a mechanism, a consequence or a narrower audience. "You won't believe what happens next" adds nothing.

## Ten hooks to adapt

| Type | Template |
|---|---|
| Problem reframe | Most [audience] don't have a [assumed problem]. They have a [real problem]. |
| Upstream check | Before fixing [visible symptom], check [upstream factor]. |
| Contrarian | [Popular advice] works until [constraint]. |
| Tool limit | [Tool] can't decide [human question]. |
| Scene | "[Common request you hear]." Every [role] has heard this one. |
| How I | Here's how I'd [goal] with [constraint]… |
| Mistake | The mistake isn't [surface issue]. It's [diagnosis]. |
| Comparison | Two [similar things]. One important difference: [factor]. |
| Decision | Before buying [solution], decide [prerequisite]. |
| Direct address | [Role] who [situation]: read this before [decision]. |

## Three rehook moves

- **Narrow:** "This matters most when the founder still approves every proposal."
- **Complicate:** "The team may be following the process exactly. The process may be missing the decision."
- **Preview:** "Here are three places to check before you change your price."

**The 15-word test:** can you say your hook's core idea in 15 words or fewer, without losing something essential?

</section>

# 4. The Right-Person Principle

A great post doesn't appeal to everyone. It makes your ideal buyer feel **unusually understood**.

Instead of: *"5 ways to improve your marketing."*
Write: *"5 things SaaS founders should stop doing if their pipeline depends on founder-led sales."*

Smaller audience, **better** audience.

| Broad | Specific | Commercially relevant |
|---|---|---|
| "Better marketing" | "More inquiries for B2B consultants" | "Why inquiries from your lead magnet don't match the service you sell" |
| "Financial tips for startups" | "Cash planning for seed-stage SaaS" | "The three numbers your board asks about 9 months after your seed round" |

**The relevance filter.** Before writing, complete three lines: *This is for… · They recognize the problem when… · After reading, they can…*

# 5. The Four Posts Every Business Needs

| Job | Goal | Example engines |
|---|---|---|
| **Attraction** | Reach the right new people | Story, contrarian, list |
| **Authority** | Prove your thinking | How-to, case study, lessons learned |
| **Relationship** | Deepen connection | Field question, behind the scenes |
| **Conversion** | Make the next step clear | Offer with boundaries, commercial FAQ |

A healthy week of five posts: **1 attraction · 2 authority · 1 relationship · 1 conversion.**

**Authority = Experience + Evidence + Insight.** Don't write "I'm an expert in sales." Show something like "We tested four outbound approaches across 137 opportunities. Here's what happened," using your own real numbers.

# 6. CTAs That Create Conversations

"Agree?" "Thoughts?" "Comment YES." These ask for effort without giving a reason, and LinkedIn has said it's reducing this kind of engagement bait. Use the **CTA ladder** instead, and climb only as far as the post has earned:

| Rung | Example |
|---|---|
| **Application** | "Check one stalled proposal for an owner, a decision and a date." |
| **Experience** | "What's the biggest bottleneck you're seeing?" |
| **Resource** | "The one-page review sheet is linked below. No email needed." |
| **Conversation** | "If you're unsure whether it's qualification or ownership, message me the stage where it stalls." |
| **Commercial** | "If you're solving this this quarter, message me about a pipeline review." |

## Turn comments into conversations

- **Bad:** "Thanks! DM me if interested."
- **Better:** "Interesting that you mentioned hiring. Is the bigger issue getting enough candidates, or finding ones with the right experience?"

Reply to the **substance**, not the opportunity. Move to DM only when they ask or the details get sensitive, and bring the context with you.

# Your 7-Day Quick Start

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| **1** | Write your buyer situation statement: "I help **[who]** dealing with **[problem]** when **[trigger]**, so they can **[change]**." |
| **2** | Collect five exact phrases your buyers use about the problem. |
| **3** | Fix your profile: headline = audience + problem + method. Run the stranger test on it. |
| **4** | Write ten hooks for one problem. Keep two. Write three rehooks for the best one. |
| **5** | Publish an **authority** post (a how-to or diagnostic list) with an application or experience CTA. |
| **6** | Reply to every relevant comment with a question about *their* situation. |
| **7** | Review: which responses came from people who could buy? Pick next week's problem from them. |
