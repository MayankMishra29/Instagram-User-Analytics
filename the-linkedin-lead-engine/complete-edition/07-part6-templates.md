# Chapter 24: 25 High-Converting Post Structures

<p class="lede">Adaptable drafting templates: a primary job, the inputs you need, a body structure and a sensible next step.</p>

These aren't performance-tested promises. Replace every bracket, verify every fact, and choose a CTA that matches your real offer and capacity.

| # | Structure | Primary job | Gather first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | The decision story | Authority | A real decision, options, rationale, outcome |
| 02 | The bounded how-to | Authority | A task and a completion test |
| 03 | The diagnostic list | Attraction / authority | Distinct symptoms and checks |
| 04 | The qualified contrarian | Authority | A common recommendation and a real exception |
| 05 | The transparent case study | Authority / conversion | Permission, baseline, action, period, result, limits |
| 06 | The revised belief | Relationship / authority | A genuine change in your thinking |
| 07 | The useful failure | Authority | A real failure and your part in it |
| 08 | The milestone with a lesson | Relationship | A real milestone and the work behind it |
| 09 | The trend, translated | Attraction / authority | A current primary source |
| 10 | The small-data read | Authority | A defined dataset you can use |
| 11 | The resource invitation | Conversion (resource) | A tested resource and working access route |
| 12 | The reasoned opinion | Authority / relationship | A claim, a mechanism and a boundary |
| 13 | The conditional prediction | Attraction / authority | Current signals and the uncertainty |
| 14 | The mistake and the repair | Authority | An observable error and its fix |
| 15 | Behind the scenes | Authority | A shareable artifact or process stage |
| 16 | The honest transformation | Authority / conversion | An observable operational change |
| 17 | The option comparison | Authority (solution aware) | Two or three legitimate approaches |
| 18 | The objection explained | Authority (provider aware) | A genuine recurring concern |
| 19 | The commercial FAQ | Conversion | Accurate offer details |
| 20 | The myth with a better rule | Attraction / authority | A belief you've genuinely encountered |
| 21 | The annotated teardown | Authority | Permission or a suitable public example |
| 22 | The buyer's decision checklist | Authority (solution aware) | The criteria that affect the choice |
| 23 | The field question | Relationship | A real open question |
| 24 | The offer with boundaries | Conversion | A deliverable service and honest capacity |
| 25 | The follow-up lesson | Authority / relationship | A previous post and genuinely new information |

### 01 · The decision story *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
We expected [outcome].

Then [event] made the plan harder.

The easy answer was [option A]. But [constraint] meant that would leave [problem] unsolved.

We chose [option B] because [reason].

Over [period], we saw [supported result]. We can't credit [uncertain effect] to this alone.

If you're facing [similar condition], check [decision] before [action].
</div>

**Next step:** Invite a comparable experience. Use only with a real story, or label it hypothetical.

### 02 · The bounded how-to *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
To [task], start by confirming [prerequisite].

First, [action]. Next, [action]. Then, [action].

Check it worked by [observable test]. If [failure condition], go back to [step].

This helps with [scope]. It won't fix [separate problem].
</div>

**Next step:** Ask readers to try it on one real item. Avoid vague verbs like "optimize."

### 03 · The diagnostic list *(Attraction / authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
[Problem] doesn't always mean [assumed cause].

Check these three signals:

1. [Signal] → check [possible cause].
2. [Signal] → check [possible cause].
3. [Signal] → check [possible cause].

Start with the one you can see in [recent example]. The point is to narrow the diagnosis before you buy a solution.
</div>

**Next step:** Offer a worksheet only if it helps perform the check.

### 04 · The qualified contrarian *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
"[Common advice]" is useful when [condition].

It becomes weak advice when [constraint].

Here's why: [mechanism].

In that situation, I'd choose [alternative] first, because [reason].

If [different condition], the original advice may still be right.
</div>

**Next step:** Ask which condition readers are in. Don't invent a consensus just to oppose it.

### 05 · The transparent case study *(Authority / conversion)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
[Business context] was dealing with [problem].

Starting point: [defined baseline].

We changed [specific action] over [period]. The client's part was [responsibilities].

Result: [defined measure]. [Other change or limitation] also matters when reading that.

The useful lesson is [mechanism], especially for [fit condition].
</div>

**Next step:** Offer a sample scope to similar buyers. Never imply the same result is guaranteed.

### 06 · The revised belief *(Relationship / authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
I used to believe [old view].

That led me to [action].

After [real observation], I realized I'd overlooked [factor].

Now I check [question] before [decision].

I still use the old approach when [exception]. What changed is how I choose.
</div>

**Next step:** Ask what changed the reader's approach.

### 07 · The useful failure *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
[Attempt] didn't produce [intended outcome].

My part in that: [specific decision or omission].

The evidence points to [explanation], though [uncertainty] remains.

Next time I'll [changed action] and check [signal] earlier.

If you're about to [similar action], ask [preventive question] first.
</div>

**Next step:** A prevention checklist. Don't use vulnerability as disguised self-congratulation.

### 08 · The milestone with a lesson *(Relationship)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
We just reached [verified milestone].

The part worth sharing is [decision or process].

[People or conditions] made it possible. [Constraint] shaped how we worked.

The next challenge is [honest limitation].

If you're working toward [related goal], start with [useful lesson].
</div>

**Next step:** Credit contributors accurately. A milestone doesn't prove every tactic around it caused it.

### 09 · The trend, translated *(Attraction / authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
[Source] announced [change] on [date].

For [specific audience], the practical implication may be [inference].

Before changing anything, check [factor one] and [factor two].

I'd act now on [clear action], and wait for more evidence on [uncertainty].
</div>

**Next step:** Link the source. Never recycle old news as breaking.

### 10 · The small-data read *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
In [sample and period], we saw [finding].

That number means [definition]. It doesn't mean [common misreading].

One explanation: [hypothesis]. Another: [alternative].

Next test: [comparison]. For now, the change we're making is [proportionate action].
</div>

**Next step:** Invite questions about method. Small data can guide a next step; it can't support a sweeping claim.

### 11 · The resource invitation *(Conversion: resource)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
If [problem], start by checking [insight, delivered in the post].

I made [resource] to help [audience] do [bounded task].

It contains [specific components] and gives you [output].

It's for [context]. It's not for [exclusion].

Get it here: [access route]. [State any email requirement clearly.]
</div>

**Next step:** Deliver exactly what you promised before offering paid help.

### 12 · The reasoned opinion *(Authority / relationship)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
My view: [specific position].

The reason: [mechanism].

Take [example]. In that situation, [consequence].

I'd change my mind if [condition or evidence].

The decision I'd make today: [action].
</div>

**Next step:** Ask for counterexamples from similar contexts, not agreement.

### 13 · The conditional prediction *(Attraction / authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
I expect [development], if [conditions] continue.

The signals I'm watching: [source-backed observations].

For [reader], preparing means [reversible action].

The prediction weakens if [counter-signal]. I wouldn't commit [large irreversible investment] on this alone.
</div>

**Next step:** Name a review date, and actually revisit the forecast.

### 14 · The mistake and the repair *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
A common way to make [task] harder: [mistake].

It happens for a reasonable reason: [motivation].

The problem is [mechanism], especially when [condition].

Replace it with [specific action]. Check the fix by [test].
</div>

**Next step:** Ask readers to inspect one example. Never mock people for making the mistake.

### 15 · Behind the scenes *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
Before we [visible deliverable], we decide [less visible question].

Here's what that looks like: [artifact or explanation].

We include [element] because [reason], and leave out [element] because [scope boundary].

That helps [person] make [decision].
</div>

**Next step:** Share a sample if it's safe. Never expose client information for credibility.

### 16 · The honest transformation *(Authority / conversion)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
Before: [specific behavior or state].

After: [specific behavior or state].

The change: [intervention], with [required participation].

What we observed: [evidence]. What we haven't established yet: [longer-term outcome].

That distinction matters because [reason].
</div>

**Next step:** Explain implementation fit. Operational clarity is valuable without a fabricated revenue number.

### 17 · The option comparison *(Authority, solution aware)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
Choosing between [A] and [B] starts with [decision criterion].

Choose [A] when [condition]. The trade-off: [cost or limit].

Choose [B] when [condition]. The trade-off: [cost or limit].

If [third condition], neither should be your first move. Start with [prerequisite].
</div>

**Next step:** Offer a decision worksheet. Don't rig the comparison so your service always wins.

### 18 · The objection explained *(Authority, provider aware)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
"[Objection]" is a reasonable concern when [context].

Here's what [service or method] does address: [scope].

Here's what it doesn't: [limit].

It's worth considering when [fit]. It's probably wrong when [non-fit].
</div>

**Next step:** Invite a specific scope question. Naming non-fit makes the decision clearer, and makes you more believable.

### 19 · The commercial FAQ *(Conversion)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
Three questions about [service]:

Who's it for? [Audience and problem.]

What happens? [Deliverables, sequence, client responsibilities.]

What does it cost and require? [Actual fee or honest pricing logic, timing, requirements.]

If that fits your situation, [clear next step].
</div>

**Next step:** Give enough information to evaluate, not just a calendar link.

### 20 · The myth with a better rule *(Attraction / authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
"[Belief]" leaves out [important condition].

In [example], following it leads to [consequence].

A more useful rule: [bounded recommendation].

Before applying it, check [question].
</div>

**Next step:** Invite cases where the original rule still works.

### 21 · The annotated teardown *(Authority)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
This [page / process / message] is trying to help [audience] do [task].

What works: [specific element and why].

What creates friction: [observable element and likely effect].

My first test would be [change], measured by [signal].

This is an outside view. I don't know [missing internal context].
</div>

**Next step:** Share your evaluation criteria. Never present guesses about someone else's results as fact.

### 22 · The buyer's decision checklist *(Authority, solution aware)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
Before choosing [provider or approach], ask:

What problem will this solve first?
What inputs and participation does it need?
What evidence would show progress?
What's out of scope?
What happens if the assumptions are wrong?

A clear answer matters more than a confident promise.
</div>

**Next step:** Encourage readers to use it with any provider, including you.

### 23 · The field question *(Relationship)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
For [specific audience] dealing with [situation]: how do you handle [decision]?

I can see the case for [A] because [reason], and for [B] because [reason].

My current preference is [view], when [condition].

What have you seen in practice? Context like [non-sensitive detail] would help.
</div>

**Next step:** Reply to the answers, and credit useful contributions with permission.

### 24 · The offer with boundaries *(Conversion)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
I help [audience] fix [problem] through [service].

It includes [deliverables] over [period].

You'll need [inputs and participation]. It doesn't include [scope boundary].

The fee is [actual fee or range, with assumptions]. First we check [fit criteria].

If you want to evaluate it, [next step, and what happens after].
</div>

**Next step:** State capacity limits only if they're real. Swap vague transformation promises for concrete work.

### 25 · The follow-up lesson *(Authority / relationship)*

<div class="post" markdown="1">
I recently wrote about [idea].

The replies raised a useful point: [question or correction].

Here's what I'd clarify now: [revised explanation].

The original advice still holds when [condition]. When [different condition], use [alternative].

The better rule: [rule].
</div>

**Next step:** Link the earlier post. Revising a view in public is stronger than defending an overstatement.

---

**The adaptation check.** Before publishing any template: the audience is specific, the evidence is yours or attributed, any promised resource exists, every bracket is replaced, and the CTA matches your capacity. Then read the draft without its template label. It should sound like a real contribution, not a filled-in form.
