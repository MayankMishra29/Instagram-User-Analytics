# My Story: I'm Not a LinkedIn Influencer, and That's the Point

Let me be upfront about something most LinkedIn books won't tell you.

I'm not a LinkedIn celebrity. I don't have hundreds of thousands of followers. Most of my posts reach a few hundred people. If you bought this book hoping to learn how to get a million impressions, I'm the wrong author, and it's the wrong book.

What I *have* done is build demand. I've scaled two EdTech platforms on organic growth. And this year I ran growth for an expert marketplace that reached **12.5k users in three months**. We onboarded over a thousand consultants without spending millions on ads. The first 1,000 sign-ups took months. **The next 1,000 came in seven days.**

That work put me in an unusual position. I wasn't just writing posts. I was working with a thousand-plus consultants, coaches and freelancers who were trying to turn *their* expertise and content into clients, and I could see what actually moved.

Here's what I saw:

**Reach didn't predict results.**

When we sourced experts, the accounts with the biggest followings (20,000+) often converted *worse* than people with a few thousand engaged followers. Many were already monetizing elsewhere. Big audiences looked attractive and behaved differently than we expected.

And one of the cleanest wins I saw had nothing to do with virality. An expert on the platform added a booking link to a single, specific LinkedIn post, one that clearly explained one problem for one kind of buyer, and got a booking from it. No funnel. No sequence. The right person read it, recognized their problem, checked the profile, and acted.

That's the whole book in one story.

## Why I Wrote This Book

Most LinkedIn content advice is built around one number: reach. Hooks to stop the scroll. Formats that "the algorithm loves." Templates that went viral for someone else. Some of that craft is genuinely useful, and you'll find the best of it in these pages.

But reach is the wrong finish line. I've written it in my own notes more times than I can count: *reach, likes and impressions are leaky buckets.* A viral post is a crowd that gathers for ten seconds. What matters is whether any of them walk somewhere afterward.

And most founders I work with don't have a lead-generation problem. They have a **relevance** problem. They're getting attention from people who will never buy, while the few people who would buy can't tell what they do.

So this book makes a different promise:

> **You don't need millions of followers to generate serious business from LinkedIn. You need to consistently earn attention from the right people, show that you understand their problem, and give them a reason to start a conversation.**

That's what I call **qualified attention**, and it's what this book teaches you to build: an engine that turns useful posts into relevant conversations, and relevant conversations into clients.

It pairs with my other book, *The Non-Salesy DM*. That one covers what happens once a conversation starts. This one covers how the right conversations start in the first place.

# How to Use This Book

**The system in one page.** Two linked frameworks run through the whole book:

- **V.I.R.A.L.** is the work *inside* a post: **V**oice (sound like a human who understands the work), **I**ntrigue (earn the next line), **R**elevance (make the right reader see themselves), **A**uthority (give them grounds to believe you), **L**esson (leave them better equipped to act).
- **L.E.A.D.** is the work *after* the post: **L**ead (create a reason to continue the relationship), **E**ngagement (start a real exchange), **A**lignment (check whether your help fits), **D**iscovery (move the right people into a business conversation).

Every post is built on one architecture: **Hook → Rehook → Body → Payoff → CTA.** And one editing checklist, **V.A.L.U.E.** (Visibility, Attention, Legitimacy, Usefulness, Engagement-to-Action), is your final pass before you publish.

**How to read it.**

1. Parts I–IV in order: they change what you write about and why.
2. Part V to build your operating system.
3. Keep Part VI (the Playbook: hooks, rehooks, post structures and annotated examples) open while you write.

**What comes with this edition.** The **Implementation System**: the Content Engine Tracker (a spreadsheet with a problem bank, post planner, proof ledger, 50-hook bank, 30-day challenge and honest metrics), the **AI Post Coach** (turns a buyer problem into briefs, hooks and drafts using this method, without ever inventing your experience), and the printable **Field Kit**.

**A note on evidence.** This book's frameworks are practical writing and planning tools, not discoveries about LinkedIn's ranking algorithm, and nothing here guarantees reach or revenue. The concept behind the book drew on a large study of viral LinkedIn posts. Its underlying dataset and method weren't available for verification, so I don't cite its numbers as proof. Where I describe what LinkedIn has said about its feed, I cite LinkedIn directly. Named businesses, figures and first-person stories in the teaching examples are illustrative unless I say they're mine. Where I use openings from my own posts, I say so.

**The one rule for the whole book: optimize for qualified attention, not maximum attention.**
